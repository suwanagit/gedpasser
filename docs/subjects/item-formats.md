# Question formats for the other three subjects

A recommendation for Phase 7b, based on the research in `rla.md`, `science.md` and `social-studies.md`. Sources and page numbers are in those files.

## What the real tests use

| Format | RLA | Science | Social Studies | App today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple choice, four options | yes | yes | yes | Built |
| Fill-in-the-blank (number, word or short phrase) | no | yes | yes | Built for numbers and expressions |
| Shared stimulus (one text, table or graphic with several questions) | always, 6–8 questions per passage | about half the questions, 2–3 per stimulus | about half the questions, 2–3 per stimulus | Not built |
| Drop-down inside a text | yes, mainly for grammar questions | yes | yes | Not built |
| Drag-and-drop | yes | yes | yes | Not built |
| Hot spot | yes, per the 2019 study guide | yes | yes | Not built |
| Short answer (written paragraph) | no | removed from the test | no | Not needed |
| Extended response (essay) | one, 45 minutes | no | removed by March 2016 | Not built |
| Calculator | no | yes | yes | Built |

## Recommendation

| Format | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared stimulus | **Build first.** A stimulus is its own piece of content (text, table, figure, or a mix) that several questions point to. Short for Science and Social Studies, long with numbered paragraphs for RLA | Nothing else works without it: half of Science and Social Studies and all of RLA depend on it |
| Fill-in with word answers | **Build.** Extend the current fill-in to accept a list of allowed words or phrases | Small change; the real tests use it only where answers vary little |
| Drop-down | **Build.** A sentence or passage with one or more menus; every menu must be right | It is the main way the real test asks grammar questions (conventions and usage are 20% of the RLA score, essay included), and it is easy to use on a phone |
| Drag-and-drop | **Build as tap-to-place**, in two shapes: put items in order, and sort items into groups | Covers the uses the guides describe (ordering steps or events, classifying evidence). Tapping works on phones where dragging is awkward |
| Hot spot | **Later.** Start with multiple choice over a labeled figure ("which point…", "which region…"). Build real tap-on-the-figure questions when Social Studies maps need them | Costly to author and review; a labeled figure tests the same skill |
| Short answer | **Skip** | No longer on the real test |
| Extended response | **Decide in Phase 7e** | Needs Jon's choice between a self-scored checklist built from the three rubric traits and AI scoring |

## What this means for review

Science has numeric questions that code can check, as in Math. Most other questions have no computable answer, so the review rules in Phase 7c carry the weight:

- two independent blind reviewers per batch, both must pass an item;
- a fact-check reviewer for every claim, against a cited source;
- a readability check on every passage;
- passages are original or public domain.

Drop-down and tap-to-place questions are scored all-or-nothing, so each needs exactly one defensible answer. Reviewers check that explicitly.

## Practice test lengths

GED Testing Service does not publish question counts. It says only that a form carries about 40 to 65 raw score points, roughly one per question. Until better information turns up, the app's full practice tests use the real time limits and the commonly reported counts, and say so. The Science and Social Studies counts sit a little below that range, so they may be low:

| Subject | Time | Questions (reported, not confirmed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Science | 90 minutes | 34 to 40 |
| Social Studies | 70 minutes | about 35 |
| RLA | 150 minutes including a 10-minute break and a 45-minute essay | about 46 plus the essay |

## Recommended subject order

1. **Science.** Closest to what exists: short stimuli, figures, a calculator, and numeric questions that code can check. It shares statistics and probability with Math. It needs the shared stimulus, drop-down and tap-to-place, which the other two subjects then reuse.
2. **Social Studies.** Same shape as Science (one practice plus one topic per question, scenarios of two or three), so it reuses everything. It adds source excerpts and maps. Half of it is civics, and founding documents are public domain.
3. **RLA.** The most new work: long passages, sets of six to eight questions, editing passages, and the essay. Doing it last means the passage and drop-down features are already proven, and the essay decision (7e) does not hold up the other two.
