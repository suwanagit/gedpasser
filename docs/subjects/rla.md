## Passages

| Fact | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mix | 75% informational, 25% literary | AG p5, p25, EH p8 |
| Reading passages | 400 to 900 words | AG p5, p25 |
| Editing passages (drop-down) | 350 to 450 words, mostly workplace and community documents, written plainly | AG p25–27 |
| Essay sources | Two texts on one issue, 550 to 650 words in total, with evidence paraphrased from real sources | AG p14, p25, p27 |
| Reading level | Like high-school courses, up to career- and college-ready | AG p5, p27 |
| Vocabulary | Words with several meanings across subjects, not specialist terms | AG p5 |

Informational passages come from three areas (AG p25–26):

- **Science**: human health and living systems; energy and related systems.
- **Social studies**: "the Great American Conversation", meaning founding documents such as the Bill of Rights, speeches, court decisions, letters and articles about American civic ideas. The guide excludes the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution as too hard to read, but allows the Bill of Rights and the preamble.
- **Workplace and community**: letters, memos, flyers, policies, procedures, letters to the editor.

Literary passages are stories or excerpts that feel complete; questions focus little on literary devices (AG p25).

## The extended response

- The learner reads two source texts on one issue, each stressing different aspects of it, then writes an argument about them using evidence from the texts (AG p14, p27). The ged.com page puts it as deciding which argument has the more convincing evidence and explaining why (LA).
- 45 minutes to read, write and revise (AG p20).
- Scored on three traits, 0 to 2 points each (AG p15, p20–24):
  1. **Arguments and evidence**: makes a text-based argument, cites relevant evidence, analyzes the arguments in the sources.
  2. **Ideas and organization**: develops ideas, orders them sensibly with transitions, keeps a formal style, chooses words carefully.
  3. **Clarity and conventions**: varied, correct sentences; the same grammar and punctuation skills as the language targets. Some errors are fine if the meaning stays clear, since it is a timed draft.
- The 6 points are doubled, so the essay is worth up to 12 raw score points (AG p20).
- A response scores 0 if it only copies the sources or prompt, shows no sign of the prompt, is off topic, cannot be understood, is not in English, or is blank (AG p24).
- Within a trait the scoring is holistic: strength in one quality can make up for weakness in another (AG p21–22).
- About 97% of responses are scored by an automated engine (WS slide 13).
