## Structure and timing

| Fact | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 90 minutes | SG p1, EH p9 |
| Breaks | None | SG p1, EH p9 |
| Calculator | Allowed, with a calculator reference sheet (SG p1): a TI-30XS or the on-screen calculator; test-takers outside the US use the on-screen one only (EH p9) | SG p1, EH p9 |
| Formulas | Given in the question when needed; nothing to memorize | SG p14 ("the science formulas will be provided") |
| Passing score | 145 on a 100–200 scale | SC, EH p7, EH p11 |
| Number of questions | Not published. Forms across all subjects carry about 40 to 65 raw score points, roughly one per question, and passing takes roughly 40% of the points | WS slides 6–7 |
| Question grouping | About half the questions come in scenarios of two or three questions on one stimulus; about half stand alone | AG p5 |
| Stimulus | Short text, graphs, tables, diagrams, or a mix | AG p13 |
| Difficulty | About 60% of questions at Depth of Knowledge level 2 or 3 | AG p5 |

Unofficial question counts seen on third-party sites range from 34 to 40. Treat these as not confirmed; the low end sits below the 40 to 65 raw points the workshop describes, unless some questions carry more than one point.

## Content weights

| Domain | Share | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Life science | about 40% | AG p5, EH p9 |
| Physical science | about 40% | AG p5, EH p9 |
| Earth and space science | about 20% | AG p5, EH p9 |

All content sits inside two themes: **human health and living systems**, and **energy and related systems**. Content outside these themes is not tested (AG p9–10).

The test checks reasoning, not recall. Learners should recognize the listed terms in context; they are never asked to write a definition, and the stimulus supplies the details (AG p8–9, SG p1).

## Assessment targets

Every question has exactly one science practice and one content topic (AG p4–5).

### Science practices (AG p6–8)

| Code | In our words |
| --- | --- |
| SP.1.a | Understand a science text: find its main idea, a restated finding, or a comparison it makes |
| SP.1.b | Work out what a term or symbol means where it is used |
| SP.1.c | Read a graph, table or diagram |
| SP.2.a | Spot a source of error in an investigation and fix the design |
| SP.2.b | Identify or sharpen a hypothesis |
| SP.2.c | Judge the strengths and weaknesses of one or more investigation designs |
| SP.2.d | Plan an investigation: order the steps, pick the right tools and units |
| SP.2.e | Identify the independent and dependent variables and how they relate |
| SP.3.a | Point to the words or numbers that support a conclusion |
| SP.3.b | Decide which conclusion the evidence supports |
| SP.3.c | Predict from data, or extend a trend |
| SP.3.d | Use and judge sampling: draw a conclusion from a sample, pick a sampling method |
| SP.4.a | Decide whether given evidence supports or weakens a claim, theory or model |
| SP.5.a | Reconcile two findings or theories: why one is stronger, or how both can hold |

### Content topics (AG p11–12)

**Life science**

| Code | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| L.a.1–4 | Human body and health: body systems, homeostasis, nutrition, disease and prevention |
| L.b.1 | Energy for life: photosynthesis, respiration, fermentation |
| L.c.1–5 | Ecosystems: energy flow, food webs, carrying capacity, symbiosis and predators, disruption and extinction |
| L.d.1–3 | Organization of life: functions of life, cells and tissues, mitosis and meiosis |
| L.e.1–3 | Heredity: DNA and chromosomes, genotype and phenotype, mutation and trait expression |
| L.f.1–3 | Evolution: common ancestry and cladograms, selection, adaptation and speciation |

**Physical science**

| Code | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| P.a.1–5 | Energy: heat and its transfer, endothermic and exothermic reactions, types and sources of energy, waves and radiation |
| P.b.1–3 | Motion and forces: speed to momentum, Newton's laws and gravity, work, simple machines and power |
| P.c.1–4 | Chemistry: structure of matter, properties and changes of state, balanced equations, solutions |

**Earth and space science**

| Code | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| ES.a.1–3 | Earth and living things: cycles of matter and fossil fuels, natural hazards, natural resources |
| ES.b.1–4 | Earth's systems: atmosphere and climate, oceans, interactions between systems, Earth's interior and landforms |
| ES.c.1–3 | The cosmos: stars and galaxies, the solar system, the age of the Earth |

There are 41 subtopics in all: 19 life, 12 physical, 10 Earth and space.

## Question types

| Type | Used for | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple choice, four options | Every practice and topic | AG p14 |
| Fill-in-the-blank | A number from a calculation or graph, or a single word or short phrase | AG p14 |
| Drag-and-drop | Ordering steps, sorting, labeling a chart or food web | AG p14 |
| Drop-down | Completing a statement, often a conclusion from evidence | AG p14–15 |
| Hot spot | Clicking points on a graph, cells in a table, or parts of a diagram | AG p15 |
| Short answer (a written paragraph) | **No longer on the test.** The guide still describes it (AG p15, p21–24), but GED Testing Service's July 2018 workshop says there are no short answer items on Science, and the 2018 study guide leaves it out of the format list | WS slide 14, SG p1 |

Short answer was gone by mid-June 2018, the print date of the study guide. The exact date is not confirmed; third-party sites say 2017 or 2018.

## Draft skill map (to finalize in Phase 7d)

Skills follow the practices, because that is what the score report and the official study guide use. Each question also carries a content-topic tag, so practice tests can match the 40/40/20 split.

| Skill id | Name | Practices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `s-reading` | Reading science texts | SP.1.a, SP.1.b, SP.6.c |
| `s-evidence` | Claims and evidence | SP.3.a, SP.3.b, SP.4.a, SP.5.a |
| `s-investigations` | Designing investigations | SP.2.a–e |
| `s-models` | Predictions, models and theories | SP.3.c, SP.7.a |
| `s-displays` | Graphs, tables and diagrams | SP.1.c, SP.6.a, SP.6.b |
| `s-formulas-stats` | Formulas and statistics | SP.7.b, SP.8.a |
| `s-probability` | Probability and sampling | SP.3.d, SP.8.b, SP.8.c |

Principles would be the 22 practice codes. The 41 content subtopics would get short background cards, since the test expects familiarity with the terms.
