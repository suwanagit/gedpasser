## Structure and timing

| Fact | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 70 minutes | SG p1, EH p10 |
| Breaks | None | SG p1, EH p10 |
| Calculator | Allowed, with a calculator reference sheet (SG p1): a TI-30XS or the on-screen calculator; test-takers outside the US use the on-screen one only (EH p10) | SG p1, EH p10 |
| Essay | None. The extended response was removed by March 2016 | AG p3 (note on the March 2016 edition) |
| Passing score | 145 on a 100–200 scale | SC, EH p7, EH p11 |
| Number of questions | Not published. Forms across all subjects carry about 40 to 65 raw score points, roughly one per question, and passing takes roughly 40% of the points | WS slides 6–7 |
| Question grouping | About half the questions come in scenarios of two or three questions on one stimulus; about half stand alone | AG p5 |
| Stimulus | Short text, maps, graphs, tables and other graphics | AG p16 |
| Difficulty | Depth of Knowledge levels 1 to 3 | AG p5 |

Third-party sites commonly give 35 questions. Treat this as not confirmed; it sits below the 40 to 65 raw points the workshop describes, unless some questions carry more than one point.

## Content weights

| Domain | Share | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civics and government | about 50% | AG p5, p10, EH p10 |
| United States history | about 20% | AG p5, p10, EH p10 |
| Economics | about 15% | AG p5, p10, EH p10 |
| Geography and the world | about 15% | AG p5, p10, EH p10 |

All content sits inside two themes (AG p8–9):

- **Development of modern liberties and democracy**: mostly civics and US history.
- **Dynamic responses in societal systems**: mostly economics and geography.

The test checks reasoning, not recall. Learners should recognize the listed terms in context; they are never asked to write a definition, and the stimulus supplies the details about people, events and ideas (AG p8). The learner guide says the same: no memorizing capitals or dates (SG p1).

Many texts come from what the guide calls "the Great American Conversation": founding documents such as the Bill of Rights, later speeches and commentary on American civic ideas, and analyses of them (AG p17).

## Score report categories

The guide names three categories and gives example skills but no weights (AG p39–40). The Educator Handbook gives a third each (EH p10). The short names below are the handbook's; the guide's own names are longer.

| Category | Share | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and writing in social studies (analyzing sources, point of view, word meaning, telling supported claims from unsupported ones) | 33% | AG p39, EH p10 |
| Social studies concepts (relationships among events and ideas, comparing ideas, arguments) | 33% | AG p39–40, EH p10 |
| Mathematical reasoning in social studies (maps, graphics, graphs, data) | 33% | AG p40, EH p10 |

The learner study guide uses three matching headings: reading for meaning, analyzing historical events and arguments, and using numbers and graphs (SG p1).

## Assessment targets

Every question has exactly one social studies practice and one content topic (AG p4–5).

### Social studies practices (AG p6–7)

| Code | In our words |
| --- | --- |
| SSP.1.a | Find what a source states and make inferences the evidence supports |
| SSP.1.b | Point to the evidence that supports an inference or analysis |
| SSP.2.a | Find the central idea of a source and test conclusions against the evidence |
| SSP.2.b | Describe people, places, events and processes and how they connect |
| SSP.3.a | Put events or steps in order |
| SSP.3.b | Trace how events and ideas develop; tell "caused" from "came before" |
| SSP.3.c | Analyze cause and effect, including several causes at once |
| SSP.3.d | Compare sets of ideas and weigh their assumptions and consequences |
| SSP.4.a | Work out what a word or phrase means in context |
| SSP.5.a | Spot what reveals an author's purpose or point of view (loaded words, facts left in or out) |
| SSP.5.b | Recognize bias and propaganda |
| SSP.5.c | Explain how the historical setting shapes an author's view |
| SSP.5.d | Judge an author's credibility |
| SSP.6.a | Combine data (charts, research figures) with what a text says |
| SSP.6.b | Read maps, tables, charts, photographs and political cartoons |
| SSP.6.c | Turn words into a table or chart, and a chart or figures into words |
| SSP.7.a | Tell fact from opinion from reasoned judgment |
| SSP.7.b | Tell an unsupported claim from one grounded in evidence |
| SSP.8.a | Compare how two sources treat the same topic and note where they differ |
| SSP.9.a–c | Writing an analysis of source texts. **Taken as not tested.** The guide still lists them, but says they are assessed only through the writing task (AG p7 footnote 3), and that task was eliminated (AG p3) |
| SSP.10.a | Read, use and build graphs; predict a reasonable trend |
| SSP.10.b | Show two variables on a graph and describe how they relate |
| SSP.10.c | Tell correlation from causation |
| SSP.11.a | Find the mean, median, mode and range |

That is 23 tested practices.

| Type | Used for | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
